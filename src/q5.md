# Question 5 — Operating Systems, Terminals, and Shells

**Format:** Short Answer

---

> Answer in your own words, 1-3 sentences each. Writing it yourself is the point.


**(a)** What is a **kernel**, in plain language? (One or two sentences. No textbook definition copied in; explain it as you understand it.)

**Answer:**
Kernel takes requests from apps and makes sure the computer's physical parts (like memory and processor) can get the job done without crashing.

---

**(b)** A classmate says:

> "I installed WSL, so now I have two completely separate operating systems, Windows and Linux, running side by side, each totally independent."

Is this accurate? Briefly explain why or why not.

**Answer:**
Not quite! It's more like running a Linux app inside Windows, rather than having two separate OS.

They are not entirely independent. The Linux part actually borrows the core power, hardware, and files directly from Windows system so both can work together easily.

---

**(c)** Explain the difference between a **terminal (emulator)** and a **shell**, and give one example of each.

**Answer:**
The Terminal (Emulator): This is the window or app you actually look at and type into—it's just the visual frame on your screen.
* Example: Windows Terminal or Terminal.app (on Mac).

The Shell: This is the "brain" running inside that window that actually listens to your commands, figures out what you want to do, and talks to the computer.
* Example: Bash or Zsh.
