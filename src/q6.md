# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**  
Prompt B

---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
Clear, structured framing (Persona & Structure). Prompt B explicitly defines a Role, Task, Data, Steps, Audience, and Constraints using clean bullet points. This prevents the AI from skipping instructions and leads to a much cleaner, better-formatted response.

**Reason 2:**  
Explicit constraints. Prompt B explicitly sets quality constraints ("concise and free of technical jargon") and tailors the output specifically for a CEO audience, ensuring the tone and length are appropriate.

---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
Prompt A provides richer real-world narrative context and tone, making the background situation feel more natural and conversational.

## Task 2 — Rewrite

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

**Your rewritten prompt:**

Role: You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.

Task: Analyse customer survey data collected from a recently completed 3-month retail campaign.

Data: A spreadsheet with ~500 responses containing:

Customer age group

Product purchased

Satisfaction rating (1 to 5)

Short written comment

Steps:

Identify which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores.

Extract and highlight the most common complaint themes from the written comments.

Summarise everything in a short, punchy paragraph suitable for an executive summary.

Audience: CEO presentation next Friday.

Constraints:

Keep the summary concise, actionable, and completely free of technical jargon.

Format key findings with clear headings or bullet points for easy reading.

---

**What you borrowed and why:**  
I took Prompt B as the base structure and borrowed the specific detail about what each data row contains (bulleting out the 4 spreadsheet columns) from Prompt A. Breaking down the dataset items into a bulleted list gives the AI absolute clarity on the exact variables available for analysis, resulting in a more precise and accurate output.
